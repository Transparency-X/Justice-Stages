# Justice-Stages

In the Irish legal system (as in most common law systems), civil cases and criminal cases serve entirely different purposes, follow different rules, and have different consequences. 

The most significant difference, especially relating to your previous question, is **control**. In a civil case, the victim (plaintiff) has total control over whether to start, settle, or drop the lawsuit. In a criminal case, the State has control.

Here is a breakdown of the key differences:

### 1. Purpose
*   **Criminal Cases:** The primary purpose is to maintain law and order, protect society, and punish individuals who have broken the law. An offense is considered a wrong against the State or society as a whole, even if there is an individual victim.
*   **Civil Cases:** The primary purpose is to resolve disputes between private individuals or organizations. It focuses on righting a wrong done to an individual, usually by providing compensation or enforcing a right.

### 2. The Parties Involved
*   **Criminal Cases:** The case is brought by the State (represented by An Garda Síochána or the Director of Public Prosecutions - DPP) against the **Accused** (or Defendant). The victim is not a party to the case; they are a witness for the State.
*   **Civil Cases:** The case is brought by the aggrieved person or company, known as the **Plaintiff**, against the person or company they are accusing, known as the **Defendant**.

### 3. Burden and Standard of Proof
*   **Criminal Cases:** The burden of proof rests entirely on the State. To secure a conviction, the prosecution must prove their case **"beyond a reasonable doubt."** This is a very high standard; if the judge or jury has any valid doubt, they must acquit.
*   **Civil Cases:** The burden of proof rests on the Plaintiff. However, the standard is much lower: **"on the balance of probabilities."** This means the judge or jury only needs to believe that it is more likely than not (i.e., 51% likely) that the plaintiff's claim is true.

### 4. Outcomes and Penalties
*   **Criminal Cases:** If found guilty, the accused faces penalties designed to punish and deter. This can include imprisonment, suspended sentences, fines paid to the State, community service, and a permanent criminal record.
*   **Civil Cases:** If the defendant is found liable, the court usually orders a remedy for the plaintiff. This most commonly involves **damages** (monetary compensation paid by the defendant to the plaintiff). The court might also issue an **injunction** (ordering the defendant to do or stop doing something). Nobody goes to prison, and there is no criminal record.

### 5. Terminology
*   **Criminal:** Someone is found **Guilty** or **Not Guilty** (Acquitted).
*   **Civil:** Someone is found **Liable** or **Not Liable** (or the court finds "in favor of the Plaintiff" / "in favor of the Defendant").

---

### Summary Table: Criminal vs. Civil Cases in Ireland

| Feature | Criminal Case | Civil Case |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Purpose** | To punish offenses against society/the State. | To resolve private disputes and compensate victims. |
| **Who starts the case?** | The State (Gardaí or DPP). | A private individual or organization (Plaintiff). |
| **Who controls the case?** | The State. The victim **cannot** drop the charges. | The Plaintiff. They **can** drop or settle the case at any time. |
| **Parties Involved** | The State vs. The Accused (Defendant). | Plaintiff vs. Defendant. |
| **Standard of Proof** | **Beyond a reasonable doubt** (Very high). | **Balance of probabilities** (More likely than not). |
| **Outcome/Penalties** | Imprisonment, fines to the State, probation, criminal record. | Monetary damages to the plaintiff, injunctions. |
| **Verdict Terminology** | Guilty / Not Guilty. | Liable / Not Liable. |
| **Examples** | Murder, assault, theft, drunk driving, fraud. | Personal injury (e.g., car crash), breach of contract, defamation, divorce. |

**Can a single event trigger both?**
Yes. For example, if someone assaults you, the State can prosecute them criminally for the assault (seeking jail time). Separately, you can sue them in a civil court for battery (seeking monetary compensation for your medical bills and emotional distress). Because the standard of proof is lower in civil court, it is possible for someone to be found "Not Guilty" in a criminal trial, but still found "Liable" in a civil trial (as famously happened in the O.J. Simpson case in the US, but the same principle applies in Ireland).
